Stage III colon cancer · after surgery

Should I have chemotherapy after surgery?

1

What we found

Surgery removed the part of your colon with the tumor and the lymph nodes near it.

Cancer was found in of lymph nodes. That makes it stage III.

Lymph nodes are small filters along the colon's drainage. Cancer in the nodes tells us cells had started to travel.

Your risk group
2

Why we talk about chemo

Surgery took out all the cancer we can see. Sometimes a few cancer cells have already slipped away. They are too small to show on any scan.

Chemo after surgery is a clean-up crew. Its job is to kill those hidden cells before they grow into a new tumor.

We can't tell who has hidden cells and who doesn't. Some people are already cured by surgery. So we talk in chances out of 100.

Schedule and side effects

Most side effects fade after chemo ends. Numbness from oxaliplatin is the one that can last, which is why the shorter course matters. Before starting a 5-FU-type drug, we may check a blood test for DPD deficiency, a rare cause of severe reactions.

Our plan

The choice we made together

Call us right away if you have

  • A fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher, or chills
  • Diarrhea 4 or more times a day, or you can't keep fluids down
  • Mouth sores that stop you from eating or drinking
  • Red, peeling, or painful hands or feet that make it hard to walk or use them
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing (call 911)
