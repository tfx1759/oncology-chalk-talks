Prostate cancer that has spread: choosing the first treatment

1

What we found

The cancer has spread beyond the prostate, most often to the bones or lymph nodes. It still responds to lowering testosterone, the hormone that fuels it, so doctors call it hormone-sensitive.

Treatment usually can't cure it, but it can keep it under control for years. The goal is to help you live longer and feel well.

High volume means spread to organs such as the liver or lungs, or 4 or more bone spots with at least 1 outside the spine and pelvis. Everything else is low volume. This is how the studies sorted men, using CT and bone scans.

2

How these medicines work

ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) turns off testosterone from the testicles. It is a shot every 1 to 6 months, or a daily pill. Everyone gets it, and it continues long term.

Abiraterone (Zytiga) blocks the testosterone still made by the adrenal glands and the cancer itself. It is taken with prednisone, a steroid.

Enzalutamide (Xtandi), apalutamide (Erleada) and darolutamide (Nubeqa) block the receptor, the lock testosterone uses to switch the cancer on.

Docetaxel is chemo, given in a vein every 3 weeks for 6 doses.

ADT plus one more medicine is called a doublet. ADT plus chemo plus a pill is a triplet.

Schedule and side effects

Everyone on ADT

Hot flashes, tiredness, less interest in sex and erection problems, thinner bones, less muscle and more belly fat, and higher blood sugar and cholesterol. Exercise, including strength training, helps with many of these.

Each cell is the extra effect of that medicine on top of ADT: men out of 100 with the medicine vs with a placebo, in LATITUDE (abiraterone, severe events), ARCHES (enzalutamide), TITAN (apalutamide) and ARANOTE (darolutamide). The triplet column compares ARASENS with ADT + chemo alone. Tell us early about any side effect: a dose change often helps.

Also part of your care

Genetic testing

Guidelines advise testing every man whose prostate cancer has spread: a blood or saliva test, and a test of the tumor. It can open extra options and matters for your family.

Radiation to the prostate

For low-volume cancer found at diagnosis, radiation to the prostate helped men live longer in the STAMPEDE study. Ask whether it fits you.

Bones and heart

ADT thins bones and raises blood sugar and cholesterol. Calcium, vitamin D, exercise and a bone density scan help, and so do regular checkups with your primary doctor.

If the cancer grows later

Other treatments remain, including chemo, radioactive PSMA treatment and clinical trials. Switching to another of these pills usually helps little.

Also

Call us right away if you have

  • New or worse bone pain, especially in your back
  • Weakness or numbness in your legs, or trouble controlling your bladder or bowels (go to the emergency room)
  • A fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher during chemo
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing (call 911)
  • Dizziness, swelling or muscle cramps on abiraterone
  • A fall, a seizure, or a rash that is spreading
